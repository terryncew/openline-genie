How it works

  • Describe a project. Genie builds a working local app and a living model of how it runs.
  • Inspect real parts. Tap a station and see its real formula, its capacity, and what limits output now.
  • Preview before you commit. Change an assumption or try an option. The preview shows the new output and the new limit. Accepted work does not move.
  • You decide. Accept commits the change with a signed receipt from your local receiver. Reject leaves accepted work byte-identical.
  • STOP freezes changes. While STOP is on, no new change can be proposed or accepted. Reject still works. Clear STOP to continue.
  • Keep the work. Export the accepted app and state, re-import it, restart, or hand the job to a different worker. Accepted work stays intact.

What it can and cannot do

It can: build a working local-first app and interactive operating model from a request; preview assumption and bottleneck changes; record every accepted version, rejection, worker replacement, and STOP with an offline-verifiable Ed25519 receipt in your local workspace; keep inspection, preview, accept, reject, and STOP usable by keyboard without a canvas.

It cannot: generate any working app for any request. It builds the project types it knows and is honest about the rest. It does not prove production isolation: the local server is a loopback boundary on one machine, not OS process isolation. It does not guarantee earnings: throughput and dollar figures are deterministic projections from stated assumptions, with a stated planning margin, not measured revenue.

Not tested: iPhone Safari, VoiceOver, and uncoached human usability. Those stay NOT TESTED until they are actually run.

Privacy

The sandbox sends nothing anywhere and saves nothing beyond your browser session. The local product keeps your project, state, and receipts on your own machine. Genie has no account, no analytics in this build, and no paid API calls unless you enable a model yourself.
